The story

Negotiation is not a talent. It is a system.

Most executives learn to negotiate the way most people learn to swim: by being thrown in. They pick up habits under pressure, those habits harden into a style, and the style works. Right up until the day they sit across from someone who was actually trained.

Nicolas Clement has spent his career on that side of the table. Across thirty years of negotiations in Europe and Asia, twenty of them based in the region, he has worked with leaders and commercial teams through negotiations where the numbers, the relationship and someone's career were all on the table at the same time: multi-million-dollar procurement rounds, enterprise sales cycles, joint ventures, distribution agreements, restructurings and disputes. As a negotiation coach he has assisted a half-billion-dollar acquisition; as an executive coach, the presentation of a Fortune 500 China strategy to its board.

What he found, again and again, is that the people who consistently come out ahead are rarely the most charismatic ones in the room. They are the ones working from a system: a disciplined way of preparing, of reading the other side, of asking rather than arguing, of holding a position without breaking the relationship. Charisma cannot be taught. A system can.

That conviction is the whole premise of NegoAsia.

Why Asia changes the equation

Negotiating across Asia adds a layer that most Western playbooks quietly ignore. Signals are indirect. Hierarchy decides who can actually say yes, and that person is often not in the room. Consensus takes time, and time itself becomes a lever. Silence means something different on each side of the table.

Twenty years of practice in the region mean knowing where the imported frameworks break, and what to do in the ninety seconds after they do. That regional fluency, combined with a rigorous method, is what clients come to NegoAsia for.

A method, not a mood

Nicolas's approach has been formalised into a set of frameworks used with executives and sales teams across the region, among them Negotiation Intelligence™, The Feature-To-Question™ Model, The Sphinx™ and The Anti-Mission Sheet™. They are deliberately practical: each one is designed to be used the same afternoon it is learned, on a real deal, by someone who is already under pressure. The lineage is explicit: the definition of negotiation he works from, a mutual effort to reach an agreement in which every party keeps the right to say No, comes from years of working with Jim Camp.

He is Global Head of the Negotiation Practice at Halifax Consulting and Chief Growth Officer of Halifax Consulting Asia, a leading European commercial-performance consultancy present on every continent. NegoAsia combines that field-tested method with the reach, research and e-learning of an international group.

Recognition followed the work rather than the other way round: LinkedIn Top Negotiation Voice, an induction into the Million-Dollar Consulting Hall of Fame, a TEDx stage in Shanghai, a visiting professorship in negotiation at CEIBS, whose GEMBA is ranked second worldwide by the Financial Times, and certification as Asia Negotiation Expert by APM, the French CEO circle Association Progrès du Management.

How we think

Three principles behind every engagement

They sound simple. Almost nobody applies them under pressure, which is exactly why they work.

01 — PREPARATION

The negotiation is won before the meeting

Improvisation is expensive. Mapping the stakes, the alternatives, the decision chain and your own walk-away point turns a conversation you survive into one you steer.

02 — INFORMATION

Questions beat arguments

Arguing tells the other side what you need. Asking tells you what they need. The party holding more information sets the frame, and the frame usually sets the price.

03 — COMPOSURE

Whoever needs the deal least, wins

Fear of losing the deal is the single most expensive emotion in business. Managing your own is a skill, and it is trainable, well before the pressure arrives.
