TEDx: Negotiation through the Looking Glass

Nicolas Clement on the TEDx stage — why you cannot negotiate well from your own side of the table alone.

October 4, 20232 min readNicolas Clement

Almost everything written about negotiation is written from one chair: yours. Your objectives, your arguments, your limits, your tactics. It is a natural place to start and a poor place to stop.

The talk above, given on the TEDx stage in Shanghai, makes a simple argument: the negotiator who wins consistently is the one who has spent the most time in the other chair — not out of empathy, but out of self-interest.

Preparation is often rehearsal in disguise

Ask an executive how they prepared for a negotiation and you will hear about what they plan to say: the argument, the justification, the counter to the objection they expect. That is not preparation, that is rehearsal — and rehearsal only makes you more fluent in your own position.

Real preparation is a research task about someone else. What is this person measured on? What happens to them personally if the deal fails, and if it succeeds? Who do they have to convince internally, and what will that person object to? What is their alternative if you walk away — and is it better or worse than they are pretending?

The mirror problem

The trap is imagining the other side as a version of ourselves with different objectives: assuming they value what we value, fear what we fear, and read our signals the way we intend them.

Across cultures that assumption fails quickly. Silence reads as disagreement on one side of the table and as respect on the other. A direct challenge reads as rigour in Paris and as an insult in Bangkok. Speed reads as commitment in one culture and as pressure in another. Every one of those misreadings costs money, and none of them appear in the minutes.

A test before your next meeting

Write down, in one paragraph, the case the other side will make — as they would make it, at their best, with no strawman. If you cannot, you are not ready. You are rehearsing.
