2. What NegoAsia does

NegoAsia helps leaders and commercial teams take control of the negotiations that decide their results. It works on live files rather than on textbook case studies, and it treats negotiation as a system that can be taught rather than a personal talent. Four services:

  1. Individual coaching — one-to-one work with an executive on the deals currently in front of them. Typically six to ten sessions over three months, in person in Bangkok or by video.
  2. Negotiation assistance — strategy, round-by-round preparation and optional presence at the table on a live, high-stakes negotiation. A mutual NDA is signed before any file is shared.
  3. Negotiation training — one- to three-day workshops for eight to sixteen participants, built on the client's real deals, with e-learning reinforcement from Halifax Consulting.
  4. Customized programs — three to twelve month multi-module programs combining workshops, coaching, e-learning and deal support, measured against indicators agreed before the start.

Engagements follow the same sequence regardless of format: Diagnose → Design → Deploy → Debrief. There is no published price list; scope and fee are fixed in a written proposal after a complimentary introductory audit.

4. Method & frameworks

The working definition of negotiation used throughout the practice comes from the Jim Camp school: a mutual effort between two or more parties to solve a problem or reach an agreement, with every party retaining the right to say No. Four pillars structure everything NegoAsia teaches:

  • The right to say No. Every party retains the right to refuse. The definition above is the whole pillar: remove that right and the exercise stops being a negotiation.
  • Preparation. Results cannot be controlled; mindset, structure and behaviour can. Preparation that focuses on what cannot be controlled hands control to the other side.
  • Execution. Win-win and compromise became the norm because negotiation was framed as making each side happy. The question NegoAsia puts instead: how much are you willing to pay to see a smile on their face?
  • Debrief. Every file is reviewed from a coaching perspective, to stay or get back on track: was the preparation done, was the plan executed, and what was the real problem.

Proprietary frameworks: Negotiation Intelligence™, The Feature-To-Question™ Model, The Sphinx™, The Anti-Mission Sheet™.

7. Common questions

Is this a training company or an advisory firm?

Both, deliberately. Training scales a shared method across a team; advisory applies it to one negotiation that matters right now. Most clients start with one and add the other.

Does NegoAsia negotiate on a client's behalf?

Not as a substitute. Nicolas Clement can attend as an advisor or as a member of the client's delegation when his presence strengthens their position, but the client keeps the mandate and the decisions. Frequently the stronger play is intensive preparation with no visible change at the table.

What if the counterpart refuses any win-win approach?

That is one of the most common reasons clients call. Collaborative technique alone does not survive a counterpart who has decided to be adversarial; the work then turns on alternatives, information, timing and composure: holding a position without escalating.

Is it only for large companies?

No. Individual coaching is designed for founders and executives, including in small and mid-sized companies. What matters is that the negotiation is consequential, not that the company is large.

How is confidentiality handled?

A mutual non-disclosure agreement is signed before any file, figure or name is shared, and client identities are never used as references without written permission.
