<p class="lede">Robert McNamara, Secretary of Defense under President John Fitzgerald Kennedy, coined the phrase &ldquo;The Fog of War&rdquo; to describe the incredible complexity of armed conflict.</p>
<p>While negotiation could be said to be the antidote to war, there is still incredible complexity to address when the stakes are high. Hence the phrase, the fog of negotiation.</p>
<p>In this first blog article we will address ten elements of complexity.</p>
<ol>
  <li>When does a negotiation begin?</li>
  <li>Compromise, the negotiation credit card.</li>
  <li>The most dangerous negotiation.</li>
  <li>Yes means danger.</li>
  <li>No is the way to yes.</li>
  <li>Shake the coconut tree.</li>
  <li>Monsters under the bed.</li>
  <li>Stop controlling the uncontrollable.</li>
  <li>Feel good at your own peril.</li>
  <li>The perfect flight plan for foggy conditions.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Introduction</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Do not accept yeses which are forced on you.</li>
  <li>Do not accept yeses you have forced on others.</li>
  <li>Yes, or the hope for yes, creates a fog which disorientates you.</li>
  <li>You must navigate through the fog to go to destination with a proper flight plan, compass, checklists, logs, and reflexes.</li>
  <li>Believe your instruments, keep emotions under control, and fly the plane!</li>
</ul>

<h2>1. When does a negotiation begin?</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Negotiation definition: &ldquo;negotiation is a mutual effort between two or more parties to solve a problem or reach an agreement with all parties having the right to say No&rdquo; (Jim Camp).</li>
  <li>No is a decision.</li>
  <li>Negotiation starts with asking someone to make a decision.</li>
  <li>Negotiations start with No.</li>
</ul>

<h2>2. Compromise, the negotiation credit card</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Every negotiation has a price which can be measured in hard or emotional currency.</li>
  <li>Pay it forward to maximize your long term outcomes (build the deal, don't buy it).</li>
  <li>Compromise = buy now, pay later (with interest = compounded consequences of a bad deal).</li>
  <li>Delay negotiation gratification (don't be a deal addict).</li>
</ul>

<h2>3. The most dangerous negotiation</h2>
<ul>
  <li>&ldquo;Everybody is somebody's somebody&rdquo; (Stefan Schiffman).</li>
  <li>There is always a negotiation behind the negotiation.</li>
  <li>The most dangerous negotiation is the one you are not seeing.</li>
  <li>Ask yourself: what am I not seeing?</li>
</ul>

<h2>4. Yes means danger</h2>
<ul>
  <li>You drop your guard.</li>
  <li>Most yeses are fake, whether intentionally or not.</li>
  <li>Did they really say yes? (happy ears syndrome, confirmation bias)</li>
  <li>&ldquo;How&rdquo; to tell a real yes from a fake one (notice it is a dot, not a question mark).</li>
</ul>

<h2>5. No is the way to yes</h2>
<ul>
  <li>No clarifies everything.</li>
  <li>Don't fear hearing No, don't fear saying No.</li>
  <li>Negotiation is not mediation (THEY are responsible to say No to you).</li>
  <li>Escape the swamp of maybe (and its swamp thing: hope).</li>
</ul>

<h2>6. Shake the coconut tree</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Get that pendulum to move (Newton's 3rd law of motion).</li>
  <li>Use reverse negative negotiation.</li>
  <li>Build cognitive dissonance with questions.</li>
  <li>Never help them answer.</li>
</ul>

<h2>7. Monsters under the bed</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Tackle the biggest problems now.</li>
  <li>Emotions eat reason for breakfast.</li>
  <li>Switch on the light (and you will see there is no monster).</li>
  <li>Use the SCORE method: Situation, Causes, Objectives, Resources, Effects.</li>
</ul>

<h2>8. Stop controlling the uncontrollable</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Don't let your obsession with results ruin your focus on preparation.</li>
  <li>Don't let your need to please ruin your focus on execution.</li>
  <li>In negotiation, you can't control the past, time, people, or places.</li>
  <li>You can and must control what you say, how you say it, and what you do.</li>
</ul>

<h2>9. Feel good at your own peril</h2>
<ul>
  <li>The Columbo effect: people who feel good always make mistakes when the stakes are high.</li>
  <li>Avoid negotiation feelgood clichés which lead you faster to the edge of the cliff.</li>
  <li>Break the(ir) record, don't fall asleep to any negotiation lullaby.</li>
  <li>Avoid the harmony trap (be nice, or else).</li>
</ul>

<h2>10. The perfect flight plan for foggy conditions</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Why negotiate — why them — why us — why now — why not.</li>
  <li>Prepare your negotiation flight with checklists.</li>
  <li>Fly the plane: execute!</li>
  <li>Debrief with negotiation logs.</li>
</ul>
